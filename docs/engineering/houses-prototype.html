A house is drawn as its plan

Every wall, door and window here is drawn from a few primitives and the cell's neighbours. There are no sprites. Each mark shows something the sim works out: whether the room is enclosed, whether the roof holds, what the room is for and how fast it loses heat. Paint walls, knock a hole in one, and watch the room's state change.

One rule for every junction

A wall piece looks at its eight neighbours. Each quarter of the cell reads its two sides and the corner between them, and that picks one of five shapes: outer corner, edge, edge, inner corner or solid. A run then reads as one mass, rounded where it ends and square where it meets. Doors and windows take their direction from the run they sit in. Material changes along a run with only a hairline seam, never an outline.

Fences: posts where a fence needs one

A fence post goes at an end, a corner or a junction. Along a straight run, a post stands every third cell, anchored to the world so extending a fence never moves its posts, and never right beside an end or a corner. A long fence reads as rails between posts, not a post on every cell. A fence joins a wall only where it runs into one, and there the wall is its post. A fence that runs alongside a house doesn't reach into every wall it passes.

Four weights, one light

  • Walls carry the heaviest line and cast the only real shadow: short, down and to the right, from one sun.
  • Openings are drawn in a medium line: a door leaf and the arc it swings through, a pane across a window.
  • Furniture is a thin outlined plan symbol. A chair turns to face its table, and a bed's head is where it was placed.
  • Material is a hairline pattern in the material's own colour. It fades out when zoomed out and becomes a roof further out.

What each mark tells you

  • A light fan behind a window means daylight is getting into an enclosed room.
  • A warm wash fills a room that has a fire. It stops at the walls, because heat does.
  • A red dash marks the one gap that stops a ring of walls from being a room.
  • Blue hatching marks floor that is too far from any wall or pillar to be roofed. The room counts as outdoors until you add a pillar.
  • The room's name comes from what is inside it, checked against rules written as data.

Materials, from hands to kiln

One wall def, many materials, as today. The material sets the pattern, the stats and the roof. It can also require a tool to build with, the way it requires one to gather. Building and crafting share one order, so a stone wall waits on a maul the way a bill does.

Rooms know what they are for

Roles are data. The first rule a room meets names it. The engine only counts tags inside a room when rooms rebuild. Mood, eras and the storyteller read the result.


    


    

      
The roof follows from the walls

      
Today a room is indoors if it is enclosed and at most 400 cells. The proposal replaces the cap with a roof span. Every wall, door, window and pillar holds the roof up for a few cells, set by its material. A 3×3 hut is unaffected. A great hall needs pillars, and a valley ringed by cliffs still isn't a house. The same distance field draws the roof: its slope is the distance to the eaves, which gives hipped roofs on any room shape.
